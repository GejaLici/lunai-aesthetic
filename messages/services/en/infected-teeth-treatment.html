What is a Tooth Infection?

A tooth infection, or dental abscess, occurs when bacteria invade the dental pulp—the soft innermost part of the tooth containing nerves and blood vessels. This is usually caused by deep decay, a cracked tooth, or untreated gum disease. If left untreated, the infection can spread to the jawbone and surrounding tissues, leading to a painful pocket of pus known as an abscess.

Recognizing the Symptoms of an Abscess

Dental infections rarely go away on their own. Watch for these critical warning signs:

  • Persistent Throb: Severe, radiating pain in the tooth, jaw, or neck.
  • Sensitivity: Intense pain when eating hot or cold foods.
  • Swelling: Visible swelling in the gums, cheek, or lymph nodes.
  • Foul Taste: A sudden rush of foul-smelling or bitter fluid (if the abscess ruptures).
  • Fever: A general feeling of being unwell or a high temperature, indicating the infection may be spreading.

Root Canal Therapy: The Primary Solution

Contrary to popular belief, a Root Canal is not the cause of pain—it is the cure for it. This procedure is the most effective way to save an infected tooth. The dentist removes the diseased pulp, cleans and disinfects the internal canals, and seals the tooth to prevent bacteria from re-entering. Most teeth treated for infection will eventually require a dental crown to restore their strength.

The Role of Antibiotics in Dental Care

While antibiotics can help control the spread of infection to other parts of the body, they cannot cure a tooth infection on their own. Because there is no blood supply to the dead pulp inside an infected tooth, the medicine cannot reach the source. Antibiotics are typically used as a supportive measure alongside a root canal or extraction to manage swelling and fever.

The Treatment Process

Treatment is focused on removing the source of the bacteria as quickly as possible:

  1. Diagnosis: Digital X-rays are used to see the extent of the infection in the root and bone.
  2. Drainage: If a visible abscess exists on the gums, the dentist may perform a small incision to drain the pus and relieve pressure.
  3. Pulp Removal: For a root canal, the infected nerve is removed under local anesthesia.
  4. Extraction: If the tooth is too damaged to be saved, a careful extraction is performed, and we discuss replacement options like dental implants.

Preventing Recurring Infections

The best way to manage dental infections is to prevent them before they reach the nerve:

  • Routine Exams: Early detection of small cavities prevents them from becoming deep infections.
  • Prompt Fillings: Treat decayed teeth immediately rather than waiting for pain to occur.
  • Gum Health: Professional cleanings prevent periodontal pockets where bacteria can hide and attack the roots.

When is an Emergency Visit Necessary?

A dental infection can become a medical emergency. You should seek immediate care if you experience:

  • Difficulty breathing or swallowing.
  • Swelling that is closing the eye or moving down the neck.
  • A high fever and chills.
  • Extreme pain that prevents sleep or daily activities.
