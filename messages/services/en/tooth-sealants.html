What are Tooth Sealants?

Tooth sealants (also known as fissure sealants) are thin, protective plastic coatings applied to the chewing surfaces of the back teeth—the molars and premolars. Because these teeth have deep grooves (fissures) that are often narrower than a single toothbrush bristle, they are the most common sites for decay. Sealants act as a physical barrier, smoothing out these surfaces to keep food and plaque from getting trapped.

The Science: Sealing the Fissures

Even with perfect brushing, it is nearly impossible to clean the deep pits and fissures of the molars. Sealants work by:

  • Creating a Smooth Surface: Filling in the "valleys" of the tooth so food particles slide right off.
  • Starving Bacteria: By sealing the grooves, any bacteria already present are cut off from their food source (sugar), preventing them from producing the acid that causes cavities.
  • Proven Prevention: According to the CDC, sealants protect against 80% of cavities for two years after application.

Who Should Get Sealants?

While sealants are most commonly associated with pediatric dentistry, they offer benefits for patients of all ages:

  • Children: The best time is as soon as the permanent first molars (age 6) and second molars (age 12) erupt.
  • Teenagers: To protect teeth during the "cavity-prone" years of adolescence.
  • Adults: Adults without decay or fillings in their molars can still benefit from the added protection of sealants.

The Application Process

Applying sealants is a painless, needle-free procedure that takes only a few minutes per tooth:

  1. Cleaning: The tooth is thoroughly cleaned and dried.
  2. Conditioning: A mild etching solution is applied to "roughen" the surface slightly, helping the sealant bond to the enamel.
  3. Rinsing: The tooth is rinsed and dried again.
  4. Application: The liquid sealant is painted onto the enamel.
  5. Curing: A specialized blue LED light is used to harden the plastic instantly.

Key Benefits for Oral Health

  • No Drilling: Unlike a filling, sealants require no removal of natural tooth structure.
  • Cost-Effective: Preventing a cavity with a sealant is significantly less expensive than repairing one with a filling or crown.
  • Invisible Protection: Sealants are clear or tooth-colored, making them virtually invisible when you laugh or speak.

Durability and Maintenance

Sealants are designed to withstand the heavy pressure of chewing. They typically last for 5 to 10 years. During your regular 6-month check-ups, your dentist will inspect the sealants for any signs of chipping or wear. If a sealant is damaged, it can easily be "touched up" or replaced to maintain the protective shield.

Safety and Materials

Modern dental sealants are made from medical-grade resins that are biocompatible and safe. They do not contain harmful levels of chemicals and have been used successfully in millions of patients worldwide for over 40 years. They are widely endorsed by major dental associations as a primary tool in the fight against tooth decay.
