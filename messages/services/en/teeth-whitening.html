Why Do Teeth Discolor?

Tooth discoloration is categorized into two main types:

  • Extrinsic Stains: Surface stains caused by dark-colored foods and beverages (coffee, tea, red wine), tobacco use, and poor oral hygiene.
  • Intrinsic Stains: Staining that occurs within the tooth structure, often caused by aging, trauma, or exposure to certain medications (like tetracycline) during tooth development.

In-Office Professional Whitening

For the fastest and most dramatic results, In-Office Whitening is the gold standard. In just one appointment (usually 60–90 minutes), your teeth can become 5 to 10 shades whiter. We use high-concentration hydrogen peroxide gels, often activated by a specialized LED or laser light, to penetrate the enamel and break apart stubborn stains safely under professional supervision.

Custom Take-Home Whitening Kits

If you prefer to whiten at your own pace, we provide Professional Take-Home Kits. Unlike "over-the-counter" strips, our kits include:

  • Custom Trays: Molded specifically to your teeth to ensure even coverage and prevent gel from leaking onto the gums.
  • Professional Strength Gel: A higher concentration of carbamide peroxide than found in retail products.
  • Flexibility: Wear the trays for 30–60 minutes a day for two weeks to achieve professional-level results.

The Science: How Bleaching Works

The active ingredients in whitening gels—usually hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide—act as oxidizing agents. As the oxygen molecules penetrate the porous enamel, they react with the discolored molecules (chromogens) that cause staining. This reaction breaks the chemical bonds of the stains, effectively "de-coloring" them and leaving the tooth structure brighter.

Managing Tooth Sensitivity

The most common side effect of whitening is temporary sensitivity. We mitigate this by:

  • Applying a desensitizing varnish or potassium nitrate gel before and after the procedure.
  • Adjusting the concentration of the whitening agent based on your history of sensitivity.
  • Using "cool light" technology to prevent the tooth pulp from overheating during the process.

Maintaining Your Bright Results

The longevity of your whitening (usually 6 months to 2 years) depends on your habits. To keep your smile bright:

  • The "White Diet": Avoid dark liquids for the first 48 hours after treatment.
  • Straws: Use a straw for coffee or tea to bypass the front teeth.
  • Touch-ups: Use your custom take-home trays for a "boost" every 6 months.

Who is an Ideal Candidate?

Professional whitening is safe for most adults, but there are exceptions:

  • Restorations: Bleaching gel does not whiten crowns, veneers, or composite fillings. If you have these on your front teeth, they may need to be replaced to match your new shade.
  • Gum Health: We recommend a professional cleaning (Scaling) before whitening to ensure the gel contacts the enamel, not tartar.
  • Pregnancy: It is generally recommended to postpone elective whitening until after pregnancy and breastfeeding.
