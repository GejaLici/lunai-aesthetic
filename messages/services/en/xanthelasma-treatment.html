What is Xanthelasma?

Xanthelasma palpebrarum is a skin condition characterized by yellowish plaques or "bumps" that typically form on the inner corners of the upper and lower eyelids. While these deposits are painless and benign (non-cancerous), they do not go away on their own and often grow larger over time, leading many patients to seek removal for aesthetic reasons.

The Connection to Cholesterol

Xanthelasma deposits are actually localized accumulations of cholesterol. While they can occur in individuals with normal lipid levels, they are often an early warning sign of:

  • High LDL (bad) cholesterol levels.
  • Hyperlipidemia or genetic lipid disorders.
  • Liver conditions or diabetes.
  • Recommendation: If you notice Xanthelasma, we recommend a routine blood test to check your lipid profile.

Laser Removal: Precision and Safety

Laser therapy is one of the most popular and effective ways to treat Xanthelasma. Using CO2 or Erbium:YAG lasers, the specialist can vaporize the cholesterol-laden tissue with extreme precision. The heat from the laser also helps seal blood vessels, resulting in minimal bleeding and a lower risk of scarring compared to traditional surgery.

Chemical Removal (TCA)

For thinner, flatter deposits, Trichloroacetic Acid (TCA) can be used. A high-concentration acid is carefully applied only to the plaque, causing the yellow tissue to frost and eventually peel away. This method is effective but may require multiple sessions to fully clear the area.

Plasma Arc (Fibroblast) Technology

A newer, non-invasive option is Plasma Arc treatment. This device uses ionized gas to create a tiny electrical arc that "sublimates" the Xanthelasma tissue (turning it from a solid directly into a gas). This allows for very targeted removal with minimal impact on the surrounding healthy eyelid skin.

Surgical Excision: When is it Necessary?

Surgical removal may be recommended for very large, deep, or thickened plaques. The surgeon carefully cuts away the deposit and closes the site with microscopic sutures. This method allows the tissue to be sent to a lab for analysis if necessary and is often the best choice for plaques that have recurred after other treatments.

Prevention and Recurrence

It is important to note that Xanthelasma has a high recurrence rate (up to 40%). To reduce the chances of the yellow spots returning:

  • Manage Lipids: Work with your doctor to lower cholesterol through diet, exercise, or medication.
  • Sun Protection: Use UV-protective eyewear to reduce inflammation in the eyelid area.
  • Follow-up: Attend regular check-ups to treat small new deposits before they grow.
