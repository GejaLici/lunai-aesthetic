What is Otoplasty?

Otoplasty, commonly known as Ear Surgery, is a procedure designed to change the shape, position, or proportion of the ears. While it does not affect hearing, it provides significant aesthetic and psychological benefits. Whether the ears are protruding, overly large, or misshapen due to injury or birth defects, otoplasty creates a more natural, symmetrical appearance that blends seamlessly with the rest of the face.

Common Concerns Addressed

Ear surgery is highly versatile and can correct several structural issues:

  • Protruding Ears: Often occurring on one or both sides, creating a "pinned back" look.
  • Macrotia: A condition where the ears are disproportionately large for the head.
  • Lop Ear: When the tip of the ear folds down or forward.
  • Earlobe Repair: Fixing stretched or torn earlobes caused by heavy earrings or "gauging."
  • Asymmetry: Correcting ears that are noticeably different in size or height.

Ear Pinning (Pinnaplasty)

The most frequent type of otoplasty is "ear pinning." This technique focuses on the antihelical fold (the ridge inside the rim of the ear) and the conchal bowl (the largest, deepest concavity of the external ear). By placing internal permanent sutures, the surgeon can pull the ears closer to the head, eliminating the prominence that often causes self-consciousness.

The Procedure Step-by-Step

Otoplasty is typically performed under local anesthesia with sedation for adults, or general anesthesia for children, taking 1 to 2 hours:

  1. Incision: Usually made on the back surface of the ear. In some cases, incisions are made within the inner folds to hide any future scars.
  2. Cartilage Sculpting: The surgeon reshapes or removes portions of the ear cartilage to achieve the desired contour.
  3. Suturing: Internal, non-removable stitches are used to create and secure the new shape.
  4. Closure: The external skin incisions are closed with fine sutures, and the ears are wrapped in a bulky dressing for protection.

Recovery and Protective Bandaging

Proper aftercare is essential to ensure the ears heal in their new position:

  • The Headband: For the first week, a heavy bandage is worn. After this, a protective headband (similar to a sports band) is worn at night for 4-6 weeks to prevent the ears from folding during sleep.
  • Downtime: Most adults return to work within 5 to 7 days. Children can usually return to school after one week.
  • Activity: Contact sports should be avoided for at least 2 months to prevent trauma to the healing cartilage.

Otoplasty for Children

Ears are one of the first features to reach full size. Otoplasty is one of the few cosmetic procedures recommended for younger patients:

  • Timing: Can be performed once a child's ear cartilage is stable enough for correction, typically around age 5 or 6.
  • Psychological Benefit: Performing the surgery before a child starts school or enters adolescence can prevent peer teasing and boost self-esteem.

Ideal Candidates

You or your child may be a candidate for otoplasty if:

  • The ears stick out significantly from the sides of the head.
  • The ears are disproportionately large or misshapen.
  • You are in good general health and do not have chronic ear infections.
  • You have realistic expectations about the improvement—not necessarily "perfect" symmetry, but a natural balance.
