What is a Dental Filling?

A tooth filling is a restorative treatment used to repair a tooth damaged by decay, fractures, or wear. The dentist removes the decayed portion of the tooth and "fills" the empty space with a specialized material. This not only restores the tooth's function and shape but also prevents bacteria from entering the inner chambers of the tooth, which could lead to painful infections or abscesses.

Types of Filling Materials

We offer several materials to suit your aesthetic preferences and the location of the cavity:

  • Composite Resin: Often called "white fillings," these are tooth-colored and bond directly to the enamel for a seamless look.
  • Silver Amalgam: A durable mixture of metals. While highly visible, they are exceptionally strong and ideal for large cavities in back molars.
  • Ceramic (Inlays/Onlays): Custom-made in a lab, these offer superior stain resistance and are used for more extensive damage.
  • Glass Ionomer: Often used for children or fillings near the gumline, as they release fluoride to help protect the tooth.

Why Choose Composite (White) Fillings?

Composite fillings have become the modern standard in dentistry for several reasons:

  • Aesthetics: The resin is color-matched to your natural teeth, making the filling virtually invisible.
  • Bonding: The material chemically bonds to the tooth structure, providing extra support and requiring less removal of healthy enamel.
  • Versatility: In addition to filling cavities, composite can be used to repair chipped, broken, or worn teeth.

The Step-by-Step Restoration Process

A typical filling procedure is completed in a single visit lasting 30 to 60 minutes:

  1. Numbing: A local anesthetic is applied to the area around the tooth to ensure a pain-free experience.
  2. Decay Removal: The dentist uses specialized tools to remove the damaged or decayed tooth structure.
  3. Cleaning: The cavity is thoroughly cleaned of bacteria and debris to prepare for the material.
  4. Filling: The chosen material is applied in layers. For composite, a specialized curing light is used to harden each layer instantly.
  5. Polishing: The dentist shapes the filling to match your bite and polishes it to a natural sheen.

Signs You Might Need a Filling

You may require a filling if you experience any of the following symptoms:

  • Sudden pain when biting or chewing.
  • Sensitivity to hot, cold, or sugary foods.
  • A visible hole or dark spot on the surface of the tooth.
  • Floss that consistently shreds or catches in a specific spot.
  • A "sharp" feeling on the tongue where a piece of tooth or old filling has chipped away.

How Long Do Fillings Last?

The lifespan of a filling depends on the material used and your oral hygiene habits:

  • Composite Fillings: Typically last 7 to 10 years.
  • Amalgam Fillings: Can last 10 to 15 years or longer.
  • Regular check-ups allow us to monitor the margins of your fillings for any signs of "leaking" or secondary decay.

Preventing Future Cavities

The best filling is the one you never need. Maintain your restorations and prevent new cavities by:

  • Brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste.
  • Flossing daily to remove plaque from between the teeth where a brush can't reach.
  • Limiting sugary snacks and acidic drinks.
  • Attending 6-month professional cleanings to remove tartar buildup.
