What is Apical Resection?

An Apical Resection, also known as an Apicoectomy, is a minor surgical procedure where the tip (apex) of a tooth's root is removed and a root-end cavity is prepared and filled. This is typically performed by an endodontist or oral surgeon when a standard root canal treatment has failed to resolve an infection at the base of the tooth.

Why a Root Canal Isn't Always Enough

While root canals have high success rates, the complex anatomy of the root tip can sometimes harbor persistent bacteria:

  • Complex Root Canals: Tiny "accessory canals" or branches near the root tip may be impossible to reach with standard tools.
  • Cysts or Granulomas: Infections that have formed a chronic sac at the root tip often require direct physical removal.
  • Fractures: Undetected hairline fractures near the apex can lead to recurring inflammation.

The Procedure Step-by-Step

The surgery is performed under local anesthesia and usually takes 30 to 60 minutes:

  1. Incision: A small incision is made in the gum tissue to expose the bone and the underlying root.
  2. Cleaning: The infected tissue (granuloma or cyst) surrounding the root is thoroughly removed.
  3. Resection: The last few millimeters of the root tip are precisely removed (resected).
  4. Retrograde Filling: The end of the canal is sealed to prevent bacteria from re-entering the tooth.

The Importance of Retrograde Filling

Unlike a standard root canal where the tooth is filled from the top (crown), an apical resection requires a Retrograde Filling. We use biocompatible materials like **MTA (Mineral Trioxide Aggregate)** or specialized bioceramics to create a hermetic seal at the root end. This ensures that no residual bacteria can leak out into the surrounding bone.

Recovery and Healing After Surgery

Recovery from an apicoectomy is typically faster than the recovery from a tooth extraction:

  • Day 1-2: Mild swelling and bruising of the gum tissue are normal. Cold compresses help manage inflammation.
  • Sutures: Stitches are usually removed or dissolve within 2 to 7 days.
  • Bone Healing: While the gum heals quickly, the bone around the root tip will gradually fill back in over the course of several months.

Success Rates vs. Extraction

The primary goal of an apical resection is to avoid tooth extraction. Keeping your natural tooth is always the preferred option because:

  • It prevents the surrounding teeth from shifting.
  • It maintains the natural jawbone density.
  • It is often more cost-effective than an implant and crown.
  • Success Rate: Modern microsurgical apical resections have success rates between **85% and 95%**.

Who Needs an Apicoectomy?

You may be a candidate for this procedure if:

  • You have persistent pain or swelling in a tooth that has already had a root canal.
  • An X-ray shows a growing infection or "dark spot" at the root tip.
  • A calcium deposit or a broken instrument in the canal prevents a standard root canal retreatment.
  • You wish to save a tooth that is currently supporting a high-value bridge or crown.
