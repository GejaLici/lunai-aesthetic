What is a Dental Bridge?

A Dental Bridge is a fixed prosthetic used to replace one or more missing teeth. It literally "bridges the gap" between remaining natural teeth. A bridge consists of two or more abutment teeth (the anchor teeth on either side) and a pontic (the false tooth) in the middle. Once bonded into place, it looks, feels, and functions like your natural teeth.

Why Restore Missing Teeth?

Replacing a missing tooth is about more than just aesthetics; it is essential for long-term oral health:

  • Prevents Shifting: Keeps adjacent teeth from "tipping" or drifting into the empty space.
  • Restores Function: Allows for proper chewing and clearer speech.
  • Maintains Face Shape: Prevents the "sunken" look that can occur when teeth are missing.
  • Bite Distribution: Evenly distributes the forces in your bite to prevent wear on other teeth.

Types of Dental Bridges

We select the bridge type based on the location of the gap and the health of your surrounding teeth:

  • Traditional Bridge: The most common type, using crowns on the teeth on either side of the gap to support the fake tooth.
  • Cantilever Bridge: Used when there is an anchor tooth on only one side of the gap.
  • Maryland Bridge: A conservative option where the fake tooth is held in place by a metal or porcelain framework bonded to the back of adjacent teeth.
  • Implant-Supported Bridge: Ideal for multiple missing teeth, where the bridge is anchored to dental implants rather than natural teeth.

The Procedure Step-by-Step

A dental bridge usually requires two appointments:

  1. Preparation: The abutment teeth are reshaped to allow for the placement of crowns.
  2. Digital Impressions: A precise 3D scan is taken to ensure the lab creates a bridge that matches your natural bite and tooth color.
  3. Temporary Bridge: A temporary restoration is placed to protect your gums and teeth while the permanent bridge is crafted.
  4. Final Fitting: Your custom bridge is checked for fit and comfort before being permanently cemented.

Maintenance and Longevity

A well-maintained dental bridge can last 10 to 15 years. Success depends heavily on the health of the foundation teeth:

  • Specialized Cleaning: Use "super floss" or an interdental brush to clean underneath the fake tooth (pontic).
  • Healthy Gums: Maintaining gum health prevents decay in the anchor teeth.
  • Dietary Precautions: Avoid biting down on extremely hard objects like ice or popcorn kernels.

Bridges vs. Dental Implants

While both are excellent solutions, they offer different advantages:

Feature Dental Bridge Dental Implant
Timeframe 2–3 weeks 3–6 months
Surgery Non-surgical Minor surgical placement
Tooth Impact Requires crowning healthy teeth Standalone (no impact on other teeth)
Bone Health Does not stop bone loss Stimulates and preserves jawbone

Who is an Ideal Candidate?

You may be an ideal candidate for a dental bridge if:

  • You are missing one or more permanent teeth.
  • You have healthy natural teeth on either side of the gap.
  • You prefer a faster, non-surgical alternative to dental implants.
  • You have healthy gum tissue and strong bone support for the abutment teeth.
