What is Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty, commonly known as a "nose job," is a surgical procedure performed to change the shape, size, or function of the nose. It is one of the most popular cosmetic surgeries worldwide, aimed at improving facial harmony or correcting breathing issues (functional rhinoplasty).

Whether you are looking to remove a dorsal hump, refine a nasal tip, or repair a deviated septum, rhinoplasty offers a permanent solution to nasal concerns.

Types of Rhinoplasty Surgery

Surgeons generally categorize rhinoplasty into two main approaches depending on the complexity of the case:

1. Open Rhinoplasty

In an open rhinoplasty, a small incision is made across the columella (the tissue between the nostrils). This allows the surgeon to lift the skin and have full visibility of the nasal structure. It is typically used for major reshaping.

2. Closed Rhinoplasty

In a closed rhinoplasty, all incisions are made inside the nostrils. This means there is no visible scarring, though it provides less access for complex reconstructions.

3. Septorhinoplasty

This combined procedure addresses both the external appearance and the internal septum to fix breathing obstructions caused by a deviated septum.

The Procedure: What to Expect

A typical rhinoplasty takes between 1.5 to 3 hours and is usually performed under general anesthesia.

  1. Anesthesia: You will be given medication for your comfort during the surgical procedure.
  2. The Incision: Depending on the approach (Open or Closed), the surgeon begins the reshaping process.
  3. Reshaping: The surgeon may remove bone or cartilage, or add tissue (grafts) to achieve the desired silhouette.
  4. Closing the Incision: Once the structure is sculpted, the nasal skin is redraped and incisions are closed with sutures.

Rhinoplasty Recovery Timeline

Timeframe What to Expect
Days 1-3 Rest is essential. Swelling and bruising around the eyes are common.
Week 1 The nasal splint or cast is removed. Most patients can return to remote work.
Week 2-3 Visible bruising usually fades. You can resume light social activities.
Month 1 Initial swelling subsides. Most exercise can be resumed.
1 Year Final results are visible as the internal healing is complete.

Potential Risks and Considerations

Like any major surgery, rhinoplasty carries risks. It is important to discuss these with a board-certified plastic surgeon:

  • Numbness in or around the nose.
  • Difficulty breathing through the nose (temporarily).
  • Infection or adverse reaction to anesthesia.
  • The possibility of needing a "revision rhinoplasty" if the initial result isn't satisfactory.

How Much Does a Nose Job Cost?

The cost of rhinoplasty varies significantly based on the surgeon's experience, the geographical location, and the complexity of the surgery. On average, prices range from $5,000 to $15,000.

Pro Tip: If your rhinoplasty is "functional" (to fix a breathing problem), your health insurance may cover a portion of the costs. Cosmetic changes are rarely covered.
