What is a Tummy Tuck?

A Tummy Tuck, or Abdominoplasty, is a major surgical procedure designed to remove excess fat and skin while restoring weakened or separated muscles. This is particularly effective for individuals who have experienced significant weight loss or pregnancy, which can stretch the abdominal wall beyond the point where it can return to its original shape through exercise alone.

Repairing Diastasis Recti (Muscle Gap)

One of the most vital components of a tummy tuck is Muscle Plication. During pregnancy or extreme weight gain, the "six-pack" muscles (rectus abdominis) can pull apart, a condition called Diastasis Recti. A tummy tuck allows the surgeon to:

  • Suture the muscles back together in the midline.
  • Tighten the internal "corset" for a narrower waistline.
  • Improve core stability and potentially alleviate related back pain.

Types of Tummy Tuck Procedures

We customize the incision and technique based on your specific anatomy:

  • Full Abdominoplasty: The standard approach for patients with skin laxity both above and below the navel. Includes muscle repair and a new belly button opening.
  • Mini Tummy Tuck: Ideal for those with a "pooch" or loose skin only below the navel. The incision is shorter, and the navel is usually not repositioned.
  • Extended Tummy Tuck: Targets excess skin that continues around the hips or "love handle" area, often necessary after massive weight loss.

The Surgical Process

Performed under general anesthesia, the procedure typically takes 2 to 4 hours:

  1. The Incision: A horizontal incision is made between the pubic hairline and the hip bones, positioned low enough to be hidden by a bikini bottom.
  2. Muscle Tightening: The abdominal muscles are tightened with permanent sutures.
  3. Skin Removal: Excess skin is pulled down and trimmed away.
  4. Navel Repositioning: In a full tuck, the belly button is brought through a new opening for a natural appearance.

Recovery and Scar Placement

A tummy tuck is a transformative but intensive recovery process:

  • The First Week: Most patients walk with a slight "stoop" to avoid tension on the incision. Drains may be used to manage fluid.
  • Compression: An abdominal binder must be worn for 6 weeks to support the muscles and reduce swelling.
  • Scars: While the scar is permanent, it is designed to fade significantly over 12 months and is placed where it can be easily concealed by underwear.

Tummy Tuck vs. Liposuction

It is important to distinguish between fat removal and skin removal:

  • Liposuction: Removes fat but does not tighten skin or muscles. It is for patients with good skin elasticity.
  • Tummy Tuck: Is the only solution for loose, hanging skin and separated muscles. Many patients choose a "Lipo-Abdominoplasty" to get the benefits of both.

Are You a Good Candidate?

You may be an ideal candidate for a tummy tuck if:

  • You have reached a stable weight (BMI typically under 30-35).
  • You have finished childbearing (future pregnancies can re-stretch the muscles).
  • You are a non-smoker, as nicotine severely hinders skin healing in this procedure.
  • You have "apron" skin or a protruding belly that does not respond to exercise.
