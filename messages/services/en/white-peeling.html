What is White Peeling?

White Peeling is a professional-grade chemical peel specifically formulated to address "pigmentary" concerns and skin dullness. Unlike deep chemical peels that require weeks of recovery, White Peeling typically uses a blend of mild to medium-strength acids to lift the top layers of dead skin cells (the stratum corneum). This process reveals the fresh, healthy, and more evenly pigmented skin underneath.

The Science: Exfoliation & Melanin Control

The "white" in the name refers to the brightening effect the treatment has on the skin. It works through two primary mechanisms:

  • Keratolysis: The acids break the "glue" holding dead skin cells together, forcing the skin to shed layers that contain sun damage and surface stains.
  • Tyrosinase Inhibition: Many white peels contain ingredients that block the enzymes responsible for producing melanin (skin pigment), preventing new dark spots from forming.

Active Ingredients for Brightening

Depending on your skin type, a White Peeling solution may include:

  • Lactic Acid: A gentle AHA that hydrates while it exfoliates, perfect for sensitive skin.
  • Glycolic Acid: Small molecules that penetrate deeply to treat fine lines and texture.
  • Kojic Acid & Vitamin C: Powerful antioxidants that target hyperpigmentation and "brighten" the overall complexion.
  • Salicylic Acid: Used if the patient also struggles with acne or enlarged pores.

Concerns Treated by White Peeling

This treatment is highly effective for patients experiencing:

  • Melasma: Hormonal pigmentation often found on the cheeks or forehead.
  • Solar Lentigines: Sun spots and age spots.
  • Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH): Dark marks left behind by healed acne.
  • Dullness: A "gray" or tired complexion caused by slow cell turnover.

The Treatment Process

A typical session lasts 30 to 45 minutes and is often called a "lunchtime peel":

  1. Cleansing: The skin is thoroughly degreased to allow the peel to penetrate evenly.
  2. Application: The peeling solution is applied. You may feel a mild tingling or warm sensation.
  3. Neutralization: After a specific time, the acid is neutralized and washed away.
  4. Soothing: A cooling mask or serum is applied to calm the skin.

Post-Peel Care & Sun Protection

Because "new" skin is being exposed, aftercare is the most critical step:

  • Strict SPF: You must wear SPF 50+ daily. New skin is highly susceptible to sun damage.
  • No Picking: If your skin begins to flake, do not pull it off. Let it shed naturally to avoid scarring.
  • Moisturization: Keep the skin hydrated with gentle, fragrance-free creams to support the healing barrier.

Who is an Ideal Candidate?

White Peeling is suitable for most skin types, particularly those seeking a non-invasive way to achieve a "glow." However:

  • It is best for those with realistic expectations; severe pigmentation may require a series of 3–6 treatments.
  • Contraindications: Not recommended for those with active cold sores, sunburned skin, or those who have used Accutane in the last 6 months.
