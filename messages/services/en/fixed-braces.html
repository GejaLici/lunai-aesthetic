What are Fixed Braces?

Fixed Braces are orthodontic appliances consisting of brackets bonded directly to the teeth and connected by a wire (archwire). Unlike removable aligners, fixed braces remain in place 24/7, allowing the orthodontist to have 3D control over the movement of each individual tooth. They are the "gold standard" for treating complex dental misalignments and jaw issues.

Types: Metal, Ceramic, and Lingual

Modern orthodontics offers several aesthetic options to suit your lifestyle:

  • Traditional Metal Braces: High-grade stainless steel brackets. They are the most cost-effective and durable option, now smaller and more comfortable than ever.
  • Ceramic Braces: Made of tooth-colored or clear materials, making them discreet and popular among adults.
  • Lingual Braces: These are fixed to the back of the teeth, making them completely invisible from the front while providing full orthodontic power.
  • Self-Ligating Braces: Use a specialized clip instead of elastics to hold the wire, often resulting in fewer adjustment appointments.

The Science of Tooth Movement

Fixed braces work by applying constant, gentle pressure over a period of time. This pressure causes the bone in the jaw to remodel, allowing the teeth to move into their new positions. Through regular "adjustments," the orthodontist changes or tightens the archwire to guide the teeth toward the final aesthetic and functional goal.

The Fitting Process Step-by-Step

The application of fixed braces is a painless process that typically takes 1 to 2 hours:

  1. Preparation: Teeth are cleaned and conditioned to ensure a strong bond for the brackets.
  2. Bonding: Brackets are precisely placed on each tooth using a specialized dental adhesive.
  3. Wire Placement: The archwire is threaded through the brackets and secured with tiny elastic bands (ligatures) or clips.
  4. Initial Instructions: We provide a "care kit" and explain how to manage initial sensitivity.

Life with Braces: Care and Hygiene

Success with fixed braces depends on a partnership between you and your orthodontist:

  • Dietary Adjustments: Avoid very sticky, hard, or crunchy foods (like popcorn or whole apples) that could break a bracket.
  • Enhanced Hygiene: Brushing after every meal is essential. We recommend interdental brushes or water flossers to clean around the hardware.
  • Follow-ups: Adjustments are typically scheduled every 4 to 8 weeks to monitor progress and update the wire.

    Who Needs Fixed Braces?

    Fixed braces are the ideal choice for patients with:

    • Severe crowding or large gaps.
    • Complex bite issues (overbite, underbite, or crossbite).
    • Impacted teeth that need to be "pulled" into the arch.
    • A preference for a "hands-off" treatment that doesn't require remembering to wear trays.
