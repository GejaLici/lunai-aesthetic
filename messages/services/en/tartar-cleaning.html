Plaque vs. Tartar: What’s the Difference?

While plaque is a soft, sticky film of bacteria that you can remove with daily brushing, tartar (also known as calculus) is plaque that has mineralized and hardened. Once tartar forms on the teeth or under the gumline, it becomes bonded to the enamel. It cannot be removed by a toothbrush or floss; it requires specialized dental instruments to be safely "scaled" away.

The Dangers of Untreated Tartar

Allowing tartar to remain on your teeth can lead to a cascade of oral health issues:

  • Gingivitis: Tartar irritates the gums, causing them to become red, swollen, and prone to bleeding.
  • Periodontitis: If left untreated, the infection moves deeper, destroying the bone and tissues that support your teeth.
  • Bad Breath (Halitosis): Tartar provides a porous surface where odor-causing bacteria thrive.
  • Tooth Loss: Advanced gum disease is the leading cause of adult tooth loss.

The Scaling and Polishing Process

A standard tartar cleaning session typically takes 30 to 45 minutes:

  1. Physical Exam: The dental professional checks for signs of decay and inflamed gum pockets.
  2. Scaling: Using specialized tools, the tartar is carefully removed from around the gumline and between teeth.
  3. Polishing: A high-powered electric brush and gritty toothpaste (prophy paste) are used to remove surface stains and smooth the enamel.
  4. Flossing: A final professional floss removes any remaining paste or debris.

Advanced Ultrasonic Technology

Modern dental clinics use Ultrasonic Scalers to make the process faster and more comfortable. These devices use high-frequency vibrations and a gentle stream of water to "blast" tartar loose. This is especially effective for heavy buildup and is much gentler on the patient than traditional manual scraping alone.

When is a "Deep Cleaning" Necessary?

If tartar has migrated deep below the gumline, a standard cleaning isn't enough. We may recommend Scaling and Root Planing. This involves numbing the area and cleaning the roots of the teeth to smooth out rough spots, making it harder for bacteria to re-attach and allowing the gums to "re-attach" to the tooth.

Maintaining Results at Home

After your professional cleaning, your "blank slate" should be protected by:

  • Technique: Brushing twice daily for a full two minutes.
  • Interdental Care: Flossing or using interdental brushes daily to prevent plaque from hardening between teeth.
  • Anti-Tartar Toothpaste: Using products containing pyrophosphates to slow the mineralization of plaque.

How Often Should You Get a Cleaning?

For most patients, a professional cleaning is recommended every 6 months. However, if you are prone to gum disease or have a history of heavy tartar buildup, our hygienists may suggest a visit every 3 to 4 months to keep your oral environment healthy and inflammation-free.
