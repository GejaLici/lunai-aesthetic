What is a Dental Crown?

A Dental Crown is a custom-fitted "cap" that is placed over a damaged, decayed, or weakened tooth. Its primary purpose is to restore the tooth's size, shape, and strength while protecting the underlying structure. Unlike a filling that sits inside the tooth, a crown encases the entire visible portion above the gum line, acting as a protective shield.

When Do You Need a Crown?

Our specialists recommend crowns to solve a variety of structural and cosmetic dental issues:

  • Severe Decay: When a cavity is too large for a standard filling to support.
  • Post-Root Canal: To protect the brittle tooth structure after an infection is cleared.
  • Fractured Teeth: To hold together a cracked tooth and prevent further splitting.
  • Dental Implants: As the final "tooth" visible above the gum on an implant post.
  • Cosmetic Enhancement: To cover severely misshapen or permanently stained teeth.

Materials: Zirconia, E-max, and Porcelain

We use the highest-grade biocompatible materials to ensure your crown feels and looks like a real tooth:

  • Zirconia: Known for its incredible strength, Zirconia is ideal for back teeth (molars) that endure heavy chewing pressure.
  • E-max (Lithium Disilicate): The gold standard for aesthetics. It offers translucency that perfectly mimics natural tooth enamel, making it ideal for front teeth.
  • Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal (PFM): A traditional option providing a balance of strength and appearance, though often used for bridge work.

The Procedure Step-by-Step

Getting a crown typically involves two visits to ensure a perfect fit:

  1. Preparation: The tooth is shaped to allow room for the crown. Any decay is removed at this stage.
  2. Digital Impression: We take a precise 3D scan or impression of the tooth to ensure the lab creates a perfect match.
  3. Temporary Crown: A temporary cap is placed to protect your tooth while your custom crown is crafted.
  4. Final Fitting: During the second visit, your permanent crown is bonded into place with medical-grade cement and adjusted for a comfortable bite.

Longevity and Aftercare

With excellent oral hygiene, a dental crown can last between 10 to 15 years or longer:

  • Standard Hygiene: Brush twice a day and floss specifically around the base of the crown.
  • Avoid Hard Objects: Do not chew ice, hard candy, or pens, as even the strongest materials can chip.
  • Regular Check-ups: Ensure your dentist checks the "seal" of the crown during your 6-month cleanings.

Crowns vs. Veneers: Which is Best?

While both improve appearance, they serve different functions:

  • Veneers: Thin shells covering only the front of the tooth. Primarily for cosmetic changes on healthy teeth.
  • Crowns: Encase the entire tooth. Required when the tooth needs structural reinforcement or has lost significant volume.

Who is an Ideal Candidate?

You may be a candidate for a dental crown if:

  • You have a tooth that is weakened by a large old filling or a crack.
  • You want to improve the shape or color of a tooth that doesn't qualify for a veneer.
  • You have recently undergone a root canal procedure.
  • You have enough natural tooth structure remaining to support a restoration.
