What is Breast Reduction?

Breast Reduction, or Reduction Mammoplasty, is a surgical procedure to remove excess breast fat, glandular tissue, and skin. For those with disproportionately large breasts (macromastia), this surgery aims to achieve a breast size in proportion with your body and to alleviate the physical discomfort associated with the weight of excessive breast tissue.

Physical and Emotional Benefits

Breast reduction is unique in plastic surgery as it often provides immediate functional relief alongside aesthetic improvements:

  • Pain Relief: Significant reduction in chronic neck, back, and shoulder pain.
  • Improved Posture: Removing excess weight allows for a more natural, upright spinal alignment.
  • Skin Health: Eliminates chronic rashes or skin irritation in the inframammary fold (under the breast).
  • Active Lifestyle: Makes physical exercise and sports significantly more comfortable and accessible.
  • Clothing Fit: Allows for a wider range of clothing options and a more balanced body profile.

Surgical Techniques & Incisions

The technique used is determined by your current breast volume and your desired final size:

  • Vertical (Lollipop) Reduction: Involves an incision around the areola and a vertical line down to the crease. Best for moderate reductions.
  • Inverted-T (Anchor) Reduction: The most common technique for significant reductions; involves an additional horizontal incision along the breast fold.
  • Liposuction-Only Reduction: In rare cases where tissue is primarily fatty and skin elasticity is high, liposuction can be used to reduce volume with minimal scarring.

The Procedure Step-by-Step

The surgery is performed under general anesthesia and typically takes **3 to 4 hours**:

  1. Tissue Removal: Excess fat, glandular tissue, and skin are surgically excised.
  2. Nipple Repositioning: The nipple and areola are moved to a higher position to match the new breast shape (usually remaining attached to their blood and nerve supply).
  3. Areola Resizing: If necessary, the areola is reduced in diameter for a more proportionate look.
  4. Internal Sculpting: The remaining breast tissue is shaped and lifted to create a firm, youthful contour.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Most patients describe the recovery as very manageable compared to the relief they feel almost immediately:

  • Days 1-7: Mild soreness and swelling. You will wear a surgical support bra. Most patients can return to light activity within a week.
  • Weeks 2-4: Stitches are typically removed or dissolved. Bruising fades, and you can return to a more normal routine.
  • Weeks 6+: High-impact exercise and underwire bras can usually be reintroduced.
  • Final Results: While the weight relief is instant, the final shape of the breasts will settle over 6 to 12 months.

Medical Necessity & Considerations

Because breast reduction often addresses chronic pain, it is frequently considered a **medically necessary** procedure. Many patients seek this surgery to correct:

  • Shoulder grooving from bra straps.
  • Nerve pain or numbness in the arms and hands.
  • Physical limitations caused by breast weight.

Who is an Ideal Candidate?

You may be a candidate for reduction mammoplasty if:

  • Your breasts are too large for your frame and cause physical pain.
  • You are physically healthy and have a stable weight.
  • You are a non-smoker, as smoking significantly increases the risk of nipple healing complications.
  • You have finished childbearing, as pregnancy and breastfeeding can change the results of the surgery.
