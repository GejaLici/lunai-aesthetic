What is Plasma Arc Technology?

Plasma Arc Skin Treatment, also known as Fibroblast Therapy, is a non-invasive aesthetic procedure that uses a specialized device to deliver energy in the form of plasma. Unlike lasers that use light or radiofrequency that uses electricity, plasma uses ionized gas to create a localized thermal effect. This allows for extreme precision in tightening loose skin and removing benign skin lesions without damaging surrounding tissue.

The Fourth State of Matter: How It Works

The device creates a small electrical arc between the tip of the "pen" and the skin. This arc ionizes the nitrogen and oxygen in the air, creating a spark of plasma:

  • Sublimation: The plasma flash turns the solid surface of the skin into a gas instantly, causing the skin fibers to contract.
  • Thermal Stimulation: Heat is transferred to the deeper dermis, signaling fibroblasts to produce a surge of Type III Collagen.
  • Tightening: The result is an immediate "shrink-wrap" effect on the skin, which continues to improve for 12 weeks.

Common Treatment Areas

Plasma arc technology is incredibly versatile and can be used on both the face and body:

  • Non-Surgical Blepharoplasty: Tightening hooded upper eyelids and under-eye bags.
  • Smoker's Lines: Smoothing vertical wrinkles around the mouth.
  • Neck & Jowl Lifts: Firming sagging skin along the jawline and "turkey neck."
  • Mole & Tag Removal: Precision removal of skin tags, seborrheic keratosis, and warts.
  • Stretch Marks & Scars: Improving the texture and appearance of post-surgical or acne scarring.

The Procedure: What to Expect

A typical session takes between 30 to 90 minutes depending on the size of the area:

  1. Numbing: A high-potency topical anesthetic is applied for 30–45 minutes.
  2. Treatment: The provider applies the plasma arc in a specific grid or pattern. You will feel a warm, tingly sensation.
  3. Immediate Reaction: Tiny brown "carbon crusts" (small dots) appear where the arc touched the skin.

Healing and Carbon Crusts

The "downtime" for plasma arc is largely cosmetic. The healing phase follows a specific timeline:

  • Days 1-3: Swelling is common, especially in the eye area.
  • Days 4-7: The tiny carbon crusts begin to dry up and fall off naturally. It is vital not to pick them.
  • Week 2+: The skin underneath appears pink and fresh. High-factor SPF is required to protect this new skin from hyperpigmentation.

Advantages Over Traditional Lasers

Plasma arc offers unique benefits that set it apart from other resurfacing tools:

  • Higher Precision: The energy is focused on a pinpoint, allowing for treatment in very tight areas (like the lash line).
  • No Heat Spread: Unlike some lasers, the heat does not "bleed" into the surrounding healthy skin.
  • Surgical-Like Results: It offers the most significant "skin shrinkage" of any non-surgical modality.

Who is an Ideal Candidate?

Plasma arc is most effective for those with:

  • Mild to moderate skin laxity.
  • Fine lines and deep expression wrinkles.
  • Good overall health with no history of keloid scarring.
  • Note: Because plasma creates a thermal injury, it is generally recommended for patients with lighter skin tones (Fitzpatrick I–III) to avoid the risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.
