What is Sclerotherapy?

Sclerotherapy is a minimally invasive medical procedure used to treat "telangiectasias" (spider veins) and small "reticular" veins. The treatment involves injecting a specialized chemical solution, known as a sclerosant, directly into the affected veins. It has been used successfully for decades and remains the preferred choice over laser treatments for larger areas of leg veins due to its high success rate and precision.

The Science: How Veins Fade

When the sclerosant solution is injected into a damaged vein, it causes a specific biological reaction:

  • Irritation: The solution irritates the lining of the blood vessel, causing it to swell and stick together.
  • Closure: The vein eventually clots and turns into scar tissue.
  • Reabsorption: Over several weeks, the body naturally reabsorbs the dead tissue, and the blood is rerouted to healthier, deeper veins.
  • Disappearance: As the tissue fades, the visible "spider" pattern on the skin disappears.

Treating Spider Veins vs. Varicose Veins

While sclerotherapy is incredibly effective, its application depends on the size of the vein:

  • Liquid Sclerotherapy: Used for the smallest spider veins on the surface of the skin.
  • Foam Sclerotherapy: The solution is mixed with air or gas to create a foam, which can cover a larger surface area inside small varicose veins or deeper feeder veins.
  • Note: Very large, bulging varicose veins may require surgical intervention or endovenous laser therapy (EVLT).

The Treatment Experience

A typical sclerotherapy session takes 15 to 30 minutes and involves no downtime:

  1. Mapping: The provider identifies the target veins while you are standing or lying down.
  2. Injection: Using a microscopic needle, the sclerosant is injected. You may feel a tiny "pinprick" or a mild, temporary cramping sensation.
  3. Massage: The provider gently massages the area to disperse the solution and prevent blood from re-entering the vein.

Recovery and Compression Aftercare

The key to successful sclerotherapy lies in the 48 hours following the procedure:

  • Compression: You will be required to wear compression stockings for several days to keep the vein walls pressed together so they can heal shut.
  • Walking: Patients are encouraged to walk immediately after treatment to prevent the formation of blood clots in the deep veins.
  • Avoidance: Avoid hot baths, saunas, and intense sun exposure for at least two weeks, as heat can cause treated veins to dilate.

Expected Results and Timeline

Sclerotherapy results are not instant, but they are long-lasting:

  • Spider Veins: Usually respond within 3 to 6 weeks.
  • Larger Veins: May take 3 to 4 months to fully fade.
  • Sessions: While many veins disappear after one treatment, most patients require 2 to 4 sessions to achieve 80% to 90% clearance of all unwanted veins.

Who is an Ideal Candidate?

Most people with visible leg veins are candidates for sclerotherapy. You are an ideal candidate if:

  • You are bothered by the aesthetic appearance of spider veins.
  • You experience mild aching or "heavy legs" associated with small reticular veins.
  • You are not pregnant or breastfeeding (treatment is typically deferred during this time).
  • You do not have a history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or blood clotting disorders.
